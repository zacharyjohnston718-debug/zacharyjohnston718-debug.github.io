FSAE Suspension App

A MATLAB application that replaced Binghamton Motorsports' paper suspension calculations with one code-based model of the car. Geometry, coilover hardware, mass, tires, anti-roll bars and component loads are solved together, so every result describes the same car, and anyone on the team can see how the car is currently modeled and how it will behave.

Why replace paper calculations

Every previous season designed the suspension on paper. But suspension decisions are coupled: move one rocker pickup and the motion ratio, ride height, travel and link loads all change. On paper, that's a chain of separate hand calculations, each for one setup at one position, and they can each be right and still disagree.

The app solves the whole chain together from one model of the car.

One model the whole team works from

Before the app, the current state of the suspension lived in meetings and conversations, and two people could easily be working from different versions of the car. Now one car model is the reference. When a hardpoint, spring or mass changes, it changes in one place, and every result follows. Teammates get it from a master file on the team's shared Google Drive. Each version is numbered, and opening a new one lists what changed since the last.

Anyone on the team can open the compiled app, no MATLAB license needed, and see how the car is modeled and how it will behave. That has cut down confusion between teammates and subteams about what the car is and what it will do.

Inside the app

Eleven tabs, in the order a design question moves through them.

1 / 10

Tabs 1–2 of 11

Start here and Settings

Start here is a built-in guide: a quick start, then what every tab and output is for. Settings sets the output folder, parallel computing, and the Fusion 360 coordinates of the front and rear left wheel centers, which anchor every input and result to the car's CAD frame (+X left, +Y up, +Z forward).

Gives the team
New users can learn the workflow on their own, and every number is in the same frame as the team's CAD.
On paper
Each calculation picks its own reference point and axis convention.

Tab 3 of 11

Car model

The whole car in one file: hardpoints in the team's Fusion 360 coordinates, coilover hardware, masses and anti-roll bar data, plus a linked tire model. Every value has units, a status (measured, CAD, lab, user, default or unconfirmed) and a note on where it came from.

Gives the team
One current definition of the car that every other tab reads, with the origin of every number visible.
On paper
Numbers spread across sketches and spreadsheets, where an outdated value can survive in one calculation.

Tab 4 of 11

Solvers

Tick the outputs you need, and the app lists the inputs they depend on, solves the suspension through its full travel and compares this setup with the saved car.

Gives the team
Kinematics, steering, static ride and travel, wheel rates, roll and anti-roll bar sizing in one run, with plots and assumptions.
On paper
One number at one pose, with ride height assumed from CAD instead of solved from the real spring and mass.

Tab 5 of 11

Tyre

Fits Magic Formula 6.1 tire models to TTC test runs, or imports a .tir file, then plots lateral and longitudinal force, aligning moment, friction circles and peak grip against load, camber and pressure. Anything outside the tested conditions is shaded. More on the tire data page.

Gives the team
A checked tire model that sets the grip limits for the load cases and vehicle calculations.
On paper
A single friction coefficient.

Tab 6 of 11

Rocker design

Tests 20,000 rocker layouts per axle inside movement boxes around the current design, rejecting any that fail the shock, spring, collar or travel checks. It keeps the best 500 per axle, then pairs front and rear and ranks the 500 best pairs by ride frequency, aiming for a rear about 10% higher than the front, following Milliken.

Gives the team
Ranked front, rear and paired candidates, with IDs that open in Candidate check and export to Fusion 360.
On paper
A handful of layouts, checked by hand against a few criteria.

Tab 7 of 11

Forces (FEA)

Calculates the force on every hardpoint of all four corners for 10 design load cases: static, bump, cornering, braking, traction, trail braking and one-wheel bump. Cornering and braking loads come from the tire model's grip limit, and every body is solved in rigid-body equilibrium.

Gives the team
Force tables filtered by case, corner and body, in the Fusion frame, plus an ANSYS script that applies each case through matching Named Selections (such as FL_UCA_F).
On paper
A free-body diagram for every part in every case, and easy to get inconsistent.

Tab 8 of 11

Keep-out

Sweeps every suspension part and wheel through travel, and through steering at the front, padded by a margin (5 mm here).

Gives the team
Keep-out volumes the frame and other subteams can import into Fusion 360 and design around.
On paper
Clearance checked at a few positions.

Tab 9 of 11

Results

Turns results from the other tabs into HTML pages for presentation and analysis: Suspension (kinematic and dynamic results), Rocker (designs), Loads (force tables) and Keep-out (the script that builds the keep-out mesh for Fusion 360).

Gives the team
Reports ready for design reviews, and the Fusion script for the keep-out volumes.
On paper
Hand calculations rarely end up in a form the rest of the team can review.

Tab 10 of 11

Candidate check

Enter a front, rear or pair ID from the rocker search, and the app builds an HTML page for that rocker: its points to copy into the Fusion sketch, every pass/fail check, and its motion ratio through travel against the current rocker.

Gives the team
A full review of any candidate before it goes into CAD.
On paper
Every layout would need its own full set of calculations.

Tab 11 of 11

Export

Writes a new car.json and tyre.json for the shared Google Drive master, plus a setup sheet for testing days: collar settings, travel, alignment, wheel rates, anti-roll bar settings and tire pressures, with blank columns for measured values.

Gives the team
A new version of the team's car model, and a setup sheet for the track.
On paper
Setup numbers copied by hand, with no record of which version of the car they belong to.

Impact on this year's car

The app now drives the suspension team's design decisions:

  • Front and rear rockers (I designed the rear)
  • Front and rear shock towers (I designed both; see component design)
  • Front and rear pushrods
  • Front and rear anti-roll bars, including the full load package for a teammate's redesign
  • FEA force tables for all of these parts

Other subteams use its outputs too: ride height for aero and the frame, and center of gravity height, mass and weight distribution for brakes, traction control and aero.

Example: the rocker geometry

Last year's rockers were designed with code that contained an error. Reconstructed in the app, they give a motion ratio at static ride of about 1.07 front and 0.97 rear. This year's rockers bring it to about 0.71 and 0.75, the value last year's design was aiming for.

The lower motion ratio fits more wheel travel inside the same shock and spring limits. Total usable travel grows from about 41 to 62 mm at the front and 45 to 60 mm at the rear, above the FSAE minimum of 50.8 mm (2 in), and the added droop keeps the tires on the ground. We kept the springs and designed around the lower wheel rate, which suited the team's goal of more mechanical grip on bumpier courses.

My role

I'm the app's developer and the team's Vehicle Dynamics Lead. I wrote most of the physics code myself, from the equations in Milliken and Milliken's Race Car Vehicle Dynamics; the anti-roll bar solver integrates a teammate's code. I used AI coding tools to help optimize my code, run it in parallel and integrate MATLAB Runtime for the standalone build.

As lead, I decide what the app has to answer, check the data it runs on, and coordinate its outputs with the teammates designing the other components.

  1. ANSYS MotionThe team's first kinematic model of the suspension, from May 2026.
  2. MATLAB modelRebuilt from the Motion model when license limits ruled it out, and validated against it.
  3. Suspension AppThe MATLAB scripts combined into one app with a shared car model, compiled so the whole team can run it.

Validation and next steps

I validated the MATLAB model against the ANSYS Motion model before building the app on it, and the load solver checks force and moment equilibrium on every body. These checks verify the math, not the car: the model is rigid-body and quasi-static, and its loads haven't yet been compared with measured data.

Next:

  • Compare predicted ride height, travel and loads with measurements on the new car.
  • A live alert when two teammates are making changes in the app at the same time.
  • Design searches for other components (control arms, steering column, uprights) on the same architecture as the rocker search.
  • A road visualization that shows the suspension moving and the tire forces changing through travel.
  • A lap-time simulation to evaluate full-car performance on different tracks.
